# **Performance**

The measured numbers: which ids matisse's encoder gives on real corpora beside tiktoken, Hugging Face tokenizers and
GitHub's bpe crate, how fast each of them encodes on one core, on short prompts and across all cores, the same again
with each competitor in its fastest exact configuration, tiktoken's and Hugging Face's Rust cores and tiktoken-rs beside
them, where matisse's own single-core encoding spends its time, what its backtracking merge changes, how it encodes
under o200k and Llama 3 beside the others, and how its streaming encoder runs in pushes and as a whole-text encoder.
[design.md](design.md) holds the decisions the encoder rests on. Every table of measurements below is generated by a
script from the outputs of one collection, begun on 2026-10-20 and timed at commit 97aa503, those of the sections on
iterations 1 and 2 from the runs of 2026-10-21 at the iterations' commits, those of the fairness section from one
session of 2026-10-21 at 89ccd3d and the scouting runs beside it, those of the backtracking section from one session of
2026-10-22 at c833c65 and the probes beside it, those of the section on o200k and Llama 3 from one session of 2026-10-22
at 2dc197a, and those of the streaming section from one session of 2026-10-22 at ab1975b; the probe's run on enwik9
named below is the exception, and the figures in the prose are read from the tables and from the archived outputs.

## **The programs**

Two programs in tools/probes measure matisse. `matisse_corpus` is the third layer of the test strategy: on GPT-2's,
cl100k's, o200k's and Llama 3's pinned vocabularies it holds the ids of `encode()` on a corpus to the known-answer
fixture's digest where the fixture names the corpus, to the ids of every piece merged with no cache, and `decode()` to
the text; for both inventories it holds `encode_parallel()` to `encode()` at 2, 4, 8, 12, 16 and 24 chunks, at the
chunkings of 4,096 and 65,536 code points and at one chunk per byte, and at every cut of that last chunking it holds the
pieces a worker keeps, its chunk and the right context segmented as a complete input, to the serial pieces.
`encode_parallel()` runs one thread per chunk, so a chunk count past 32,768 is skipped with a line saying so, and the
cut check covers it alone; a thread per chunk at every cut of `munch-cpp@15b2863c` under cl100k's factorization
inventory, 559,642 chunks, fails on this machine with `std::system_error` when a thread cannot be started. On enwik9 the
4,096-code-point chunking is 243,535 chunks, and 136,695,218 to 148,173,524 cuts per tokenizer and inventory are
checked. The probe prints each corpus's ids line, the count and the SHA-256 of the ids as little-endian 32-bit words,
which every other encoder's ids are compared by, and the certified-anchor supply.

`matisse_bench` times `encode()`, the same encoding with no cache, the segmentation alone, one call per prompt, with a
piece cache of its own and with one cache kept across the calls, `encode_parallel()` at each worker count on one long
text and `encode()` over a batch of documents spread over a pool of threads that each take the next document from a
shared counter and keep one cache across the documents they take. Every row runs one pass that warms the caches and
gives the result every timed pass must reproduce; that pass is held to `encode()`'s ids in the rows of the encoding with
no cache, of the prompts with a kept cache, of `encode_parallel()` and of the batch, and every mode prints the ids line
of `encode()`, so no figure is taken of an encoding other than the one whose ids are compared. Both programs run as
CTest entries when `-DMATISSE_VOCAB_DIR` names the vocabulary directory, the bench for one pass of the single mode's
rows on the validator's sample, and `-DMATISSE_CORPUS_DIR` adds the probe on the three corpora of the test strategy.

The other encoders run from harnesses kept beside the corpora outside the repository, so that nothing a vendor ships
enters it, and print rows and ids lines in the same format:

- tiktoken 0.14.0 and numpy 2.5.4 from PyPI, under Python 3.12.3. Its encodings are built by its own `openai_public`
  constructors from the pinned rank files, served through `TIKTOKEN_CACHE_DIR` and held there to the digests tiktoken
  pins; Llama 3's is built from llama3.tiktoken with the pattern of Meta's tokenizer.py. A long text is timed through
  `encode_to_numpy(text, disallowed_special=())`, the Rust core's encoding returned as one buffer with no Python list,
  the text a Python string before the clock starts. A prompt is timed through `encode_ordinary()`, the per-call
  interface, its Python list included. A batch is `encode_ordinary_batch(documents, num_threads=N)`, which maps
  `encode_ordinary()` over the documents on a `ThreadPoolExecutor` of N threads, given 10,000 documents a call, the time
  summed over the calls; its Rust binding encodes with the interpreter lock released and returns the ids as a Python
  list.
- Hugging Face tokenizers 0.23.3 from PyPI, loading the tokenizer.json of each tokenizer, pinned by the digests below,
  with `encode_special_tokens` set so that a special token's spelling is text, as in the other encoders. A long text and
  a prompt are timed through `encode(text, add_special_tokens=False)`, which builds the whole `Encoding` with its tokens
  and offsets. A batch is `encode_batch()` with `RAYON_NUM_THREADS` set to the thread count, whose binding encodes the
  documents in parallel with the interpreter lock released, given 10,000 documents a call as tiktoken is. The
  Xenova/gpt-4 conversion carries cl100k's pattern as tiktoken shipped it before its possessive rewrite.
- bpe 0.2.3 and bpe-openai 0.3.2 from crates.io, built with Rust 1.99.1 at opt-level 3 with link-time optimization and
  one codegen unit, the versions pinned by the harness's Cargo.lock. cl100k and o200k are bpe-openai's own tokenizers,
  whose bundled rank files have the pinned files' SHA-256; GPT-2 is built from GPT-2's ranks as tiktoken reads them,
  whose SHA-256 is tiktoken's r50k_base digest, under GPT-2's pattern with its look-ahead written as bpe-openai writes
  cl100k's. Llama 3 is left out, since the crate requires every token to encode to itself and 588 of Llama 3's do not,
  as design.md counts them. Every call is `Tokenizer::encode()`, and a batch is the same pool of threads as matisse's,
  written in the harness.

## **The machine**

An Intel i9-12900K with 47 GiB of memory, under WSL2 (Linux 6.18.54.1-microsoft-standard-WSL2, Ubuntu 24.04.4). Its 8
performance cores of two threads and 8 efficiency cores, 24 logical processors, appear to WSL2 as 12 cores of two
threads, and WSL2 exposes no CPU frequency governor. matisse is built with GCC 13.3 at `-O2`, the repository's Release
flags. The machine was otherwise idle for the whole collection, and the timing passes ran back to back. Every
single-thread row ran under `taskset -c 2`; under WSL2 that pins the process to one virtual processor, which Hyper-V may
place on any host core, performance or efficiency, so the single-thread figures carry that spread, and the parallel rows
ran unpinned. The corpora are read into memory before any clock starts. The figures compare within this collection;
rerun on your own machine before quoting any of them.

## **Exactness**

Every encoder's ids were compared by digest on every corpus it ran on, and none differs; the ids on the prompts, the
mixed file and the pages, and matisse's on the three corpora of the test strategy, come from the ids lines of the timed
runs. matisse's ids on the three corpora of the test strategy are the known-answer fixture's. Hugging Face could not
encode enwik9 whole, its process killed with 46 GiB resident while it built the `Encoding`, and was not run on the mixed
file, twice that size; its ids of enwik9's pages, concatenated, have enwik9's digest, so every page begins at a piece
boundary of every pattern, and the batch covers enwik9. Under o200k and Llama 3 matisse's ids equal every other
encoder's on every corpus as well. Each engine carries Unicode tables of its own and matisse pins Unicode 15.0.0, and on
these corpora the difference changes no id.

### **The merge's constants**

A piece of at most 128 bytes is merged by a scan of its parts, held in an array on the stack, each part with the rank
and product of its join with the next: the scan finds the least rank from the left, so the leftmost on a tie, makes that
join and looks up the two pairs the joined part makes, which is the join the heap makes and the two pairs it pushes. A
longer piece is merged by the heap as before, its entries and the nodes of its list in 32-bit fields, the entries half
their size, and the merge refuses a piece of 2^32 bytes or more. The references hold both paths, a quarter of their
20,000 random pieces of every vocabulary cut at 100 to 300 bytes and every random table with one piece of 100 to 160

### **One automaton for the segmentation**

The segmenter walks one DFA, the product of the alternatives' automata, where it ran each alternative's in turn at every
boundary until one accepted. A state of the product is each alternative's state and the first alternative that has
accepted so far, every later one stopped, and it accepts where that alternative accepts; when the walk stops, the first
that has accepted is the first alternative with an accept anywhere, and from its first accept on the product accepts
exactly where it does, so munch's `run()` of the product gives that alternative's longest accept. The product has 36
states for GPT-2's twelve alternatives and 33 for cl100k's eight, and the segmentation fixture and the corpus digests

### **The configurations**

bpe 0.2.3 has three encoders that give byte-pair encoding's ids for a whole input, `encode_via_backtracking()`, the
backtracking encoder over the longest-match automaton, `encode_via_bitfield()`, a heap of pairs over a bitfield of token
boundaries, and `encode_via_table()`, the dynamic programme over every prefix; `encode_greedy()` and `encode_minimal()`
give other encodings, and the appendable, prependable and interval encoders count tokens. bpe-openai 0.3.2's
`Tokenizer::encode()`, which the earlier sections time, splits the text with its pre-tokenizer, one regex-automata
search over the pattern's alternatives with the look-ahead alternative written as a second pattern whose last character
it drops, encodes each piece by backtracking and collects the pieces' vectors into one. The harness also times the same
pieces, each encoded by one of the three encoders and appended to one vector reserved for a token every two bytes.
Backtracking into the reserved vector is the fastest by the geometric mean of its median rates and on 16 of the 18 cells
of the corpora under 10 MB, `Tokenizer::encode()` on the other two and on enwik8, and bitfield and table are slower on
every cell. No buffer can be kept across pieces or calls through the crate's public interface: every encoder's call
allocates its vector of ids and its working state, a bitfield for backtracking and for the heap and a token for every
byte for the table, and the backtracking encoder's state is private to the crate. The crate rebuilds the merges from the
tokens alone, which gives rank-order byte-pair encoding's ids for a table in which each token's two constituents come
before it; a table outside that earlier-constituent order is where its ids can differ. cl100k's and o200k's tables are
inside that order, so the difference cannot reach them, and under GPT-2 every digest agrees as well. The harness, and
every crate it builds, is compiled by Rust 1.99.1 at opt-level 3 with link-time optimization and one codegen unit, and
the C parts of Hugging Face's crate at `-O3`.

tiktoken 0.14.0 is the newest release on PyPI. From Python, `encode_ordinary()` returns a list, `encode()` with
`disallowed_special=()` runs the same encoding after a search for special tokens and returns a list, and
`encode_to_numpy()` runs `encode()`'s and returns its ids as one buffer that numpy wraps. `encode_to_numpy()` is the
fastest on all 18 cells and on the prompts. Its binding releases the interpreter lock around the encoding, `py.detach()`
in 0.14.0's py.rs, and holds it while it reads the text and builds the buffer, where `encode_ordinary()` builds a list.
`encode_ordinary_batch()` maps `encode_ordinary()` over a `ThreadPoolExecutor` of `num_threads` threads, 8 unless given
and 24 here, that each call starts; the harness also maps `encode_to_numpy()` over one pool of 24 threads that it keeps
across the calls. tiktoken's core is the sdist's src/lib.rs built as a Rust library without its Python feature, its
`CoreBPE` built from the pinned rank files under openai_public's patterns and called through `encode_ordinary()` from
Rust threads, the batch on the same pool as matisse's; its fancy-regex is the wheel's 0.19.0 and its regex-automata the
wheel's 0.4.18, so it is the wheel's engine without the interpreter. tiktoken-rs 0.12.1, the newest on crates.io,
carries a copy of the same core and its own rank files, which are the pinned ones byte for byte.

Hugging Face tokenizers 0.23.3 is the newest release on PyPI; crates.io carries release candidates of 1.0.0, which were
not timed. From Python, `encode()` builds an `Encoding` with character offsets, and `encode_batch_fast()` builds it with
no offsets; given one text, it encodes that text alone, and it is the faster on all 18 cells. The crate is tokenizers
0.23.2, the version the 0.23.3 sdist builds its binding from, built from that sdist with the binding's features and
called through `Tokenizer::encode_fast()`, which gives no offsets, 1.13 to 2.10 times as fast as Python's
`encode_batch_fast()` under GPT-2 and 1.17 to 1.62 under cl100k. For a batch the crate's `encode_batch_fast()` encodes
the documents on Rayon's pool of `RAYON_NUM_THREADS` threads, and `encode_fast()` per document on the harness's pool is
faster. Neither tokenizer.json has a normalizer, and cl100k's has no post-processor; among the work Hugging Face does

## **Backtracking**

The merge backtracks (c833c65) on a definition that meets the condition [design.md](design.md) states, which GPT-2's,
cl100k's and o200k's files meet: a piece longer than 128 bytes, or one at least half of whose bytes lie outside ASCII,
is merged by laying the longest token that begins the rest of it, read from a trie, holding each token to the one before
it by a test of their pair and taking a token back when none fits, the bpe crate's method, and every other piece by the
scan; a definition that does not meet it keeps the scan and the heap. The tables of this section are generated by
scripts from the outputs of one session of 2026-10-22 and of the alternations and probes beside it, on the machine above
with the corpora, passes and pinning above, a build of c833c65 beside one of the commit before it, 316533f, and the bpe
crate's two fastest exact configurations of the fairness section, `Tokenizer::encode()` and its pieces by
`encode_via_backtracking()`, rerun beside them. Every one of the session's 116 ids lines equals the digest of the
exactness table, and before every stage the processes above 1 per cent of a processor, by the share of each one's
lifetime, were recorded, the only one found, at 3 per cent, being the idle program that started the session. After the
commit the test suite passed and `matisse_corpus` held the ids on every corpus under 10 MB and on enwik8 to the digests
of the exactness table. Three mutants of the backtracking fail tests, each test run alone: a pair test that undoes the
left token's join on a tie of orders fails 7, the backtracking's references on the random tables and on GPT-2's,
cl100k's and o200k's files and the merge's on the random tables and on GPT-2's and cl100k's, each by a crash; a search
that lays the last token tried where it should take a token back fails 8, those and the merge's reference on o200k's
file; and a condition that every table meets fails 6, the three tables that do not meet it and Llama 3's file being
taken to meet it and the references on the random tables failing. A cell is the median MiB/s, and the gain is the after
over the before.

In pushes of 4 KiB the stream runs at 18.4 to 46.1 MiB/s under GPT-2, 11.7 to 46.2 under cl100k, 8.9 to 42.8 under o200k
and 11.4 to 49.9 under Llama 3, a fifth to over half of `encode()`'s rate and under Llama 3 on Chinese 0.87 of it. In
pushes of 64 bytes it runs at 1.5 to 24.8 MiB/s under GPT-2 and at 0.3 to 15.3 under the other three, and below 1 MiB/s
on munch's Python sources and on Swedish under cl100k, o200k and Llama 3 and on Chinese under o200k and Llama 3. Every
push runs the boundary tests of every live run's open piece, so the smaller the pushes the more tests a text takes: a
boundary the pre-filter cannot clear is merged against the continuations that can straddle the piece's end, and the test
of one that holds runs through all of them.

As a whole-text encoder, one push and `finish()`, the stream is not competitive with `encode()`: it runs at 0.24 to 0.66
times `encode()`'s rate on every tokenizer and corpus but one, 19.9 to 51.0 MiB/s, the exception being Chinese under
Llama 3, where `encode()` itself runs at 17.2 MiB/s, its pieces merged by the scan and the heap as the section on o200k
and Llama 3 shows, and the stream at 0.94 of it. The stream merges every piece `encode()` merges, with the same merge
and a piece cache of its own, and does more besides: the steps of every live run of the verifier at each code point, the
tree of their markings and the closing of each piece as it becomes stable, work a whole text, whose continuation is
known to be empty, does not need.

## **Reproducing**

The corpora are built by `make_corpora.py fetch` and `derive` in the corpus directory, whose README is the recipe, and
the harnesses, their lock files, the collection script and the scripts that read its outputs into these tables are kept
beside them. The matisse side, with `VOCAB` the directory of the pinned vocabulary files, `CORPORA` the small corpora
and `LARGE` the GB-sized ones:

```
$ cmake -S . -B build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DMATISSE_VOCAB_DIR=VOCAB -DMATISSE_CORPUS_DIR=CORPORA
$ cmake --build build -j 8
$ ctest --test-dir build --timeout 300 -R 'matisse_corpus|matisse_bench'
$ ./build/tools/probes/matisse_corpus VOCAB CORPORA/gutenberg.txt CORPORA/wiki-zh.txt LARGE/enwik8
$ taskset -c 2 ./build/tools/probes/matisse_bench single VOCAB gpt2,cl100k 15 CORPORA/twitter.json
$ taskset -c 2 ./build/tools/probes/matisse_bench prompts VOCAB gpt2,cl100k 5 CORPORA/prompts.txt
$ ./build/tools/probes/matisse_bench parallel VOCAB gpt2,cl100k 3 LARGE/enwik9
$ taskset -c 2 ./build/tools/probes/matisse_bench batch VOCAB gpt2,cl100k 3 LARGE/enwik9-pages.nul 1
$ ./build/tools/probes/matisse_bench batch VOCAB gpt2,cl100k 3 LARGE/enwik9-pages.nul 24
```

The other encoders' rows come from the harnesses, `vendors.py MODE TOKENIZERS PASSES FILE [THREADS]` for tiktoken and
Hugging Face, with `RAYON_NUM_THREADS` set to the thread count and `VENDORS` naming which of the two run, and
`vendor_bench MODE TOKENIZERS PASSES FILE [THREADS]` for the bpe crate and, in the fairness section, tiktoken's core,
tiktoken-rs and Hugging Face's crate, each reading the vocabulary files from the paths it names; their
`digests TOKENIZERS FILE...` mode prints the ids lines alone, and the whole collection is one script, `campaign.sh`,
which records the machine first as munch's `collect.sh` does. The runs of iteration 1 are `iteration1.sh`, which times
one build, and `final1.sh`, the closing collection with tiktoken and the bpe crate, both kept beside `campaign.sh`, and
`doc_iteration1.py` and `final1_tables.py` write their tables. Iteration 2's are `iteration2.sh` and `final2.sh` beside
them, with `tune2.sh`, which alternated the builds of the scan's thresholds, and `gxx-O3.sh`, the compiler the `-O3`
build was configured with, and `doc_iteration2.py`, `final2_tables.py` and `profile_table2.py` write its tables; the
mutants and the product's state counts are archived with the runs. The archived outputs predate a rewrite of the history
and name the commits they timed by earlier hashes; the commits cited here hold the same sources.

The fairness section's session is `fairness.sh`, kept beside `campaign.sh`, and `fairness_tables.py` writes its tables
from that session and from the scouting runs archived with it. `vendor_bench` runs the encoders `ENCODERS` names and
`vendors.py` times the calls `CALLS` names, each harness's header listing the names it takes; tiktoken's core and
Hugging Face's crate are built by the Rust harness from the sdists of tiktoken 0.14.0 and tokenizers 0.23.3 from PyPI,
whose SHA-256 are `231dec90efcdccf1b565a1416107736f1e09b1a08fe736ef9d6363e626d03874` and
`cded33237c77caeef62944d32aa9a7ef42bdce2b3497e18d137e072a8c4be438`, kept outside the repository as the harnesses are,
and tiktoken-rs 0.12.1 comes from crates.io, every version pinned by the harness's Cargo.lock. A row of each kind and
the native builds:

```
$ ENCODERS=bpe,bpe-backtrack,bpe-bitfield,bpe-table,tiktoken,tiktoken-rs,hf \
      taskset -c 2 vendor_bench single gpt2,cl100k 15 CORPORA/twitter.json
$ VENDORS=tiktoken CALLS=encode_to_numpy,encode_ordinary \
      taskset -c 2 python -I vendors.py single gpt2,cl100k 15 CORPORA/twitter.json
$ RAYON_NUM_THREADS=24 ENCODERS=hf,hf-rayon vendor_bench batch gpt2,cl100k 3 LARGE/enwik9-pages.nul 24
$ RAYON_NUM_THREADS=24 VENDORS=tiktoken CALLS=numpy_pool python -I vendors.py batch gpt2,cl100k 3 \
      LARGE/enwik9-pages.nul 24
$ RUSTFLAGS="-C target-cpu=native" CFLAGS="-O3 -march=native" CXXFLAGS="-O3 -march=native" cargo build --release
$ cmake -S . -B build-native -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DCMAKE_CXX_FLAGS=-march=native -DMATISSE_VOCAB_DIR=VOCAB
```

The backtracking section's session is `backtracking.sh BEFORE AFTER OUTPUT_DIR`, kept beside `campaign.sh`, which runs
the two builds' `matisse_bench` and `vendor_bench` with `ENCODERS=bpe,bpe-backtrack`, and `batch-repeat.sh` beside its
outputs alternated the batch at 24 threads; `backtracking_tables.py` writes its tables. The probes of where the
backtracking pays are builds of c833c65's tree with `partner-bits.patch` and `threshold-sweep.patch`, which routes every
piece of a threshold or more to the backtracking and is timed by `threshold-sweep.sh`, and on top of them
`piece-costs-short.patch`, which times the scan and the backtracking on every piece of up to 128 bytes, and
`piece-costs-long.patch`, the heap and the backtracking on the longer ones, both run by `piece-costs.sh`;
`routing_tables.py` writes their table and figures, and the mutants' patches and logs are archived with the session.

The session of the section on o200k and Llama 3 is `o200k_llama3.sh MATISSE OUTPUT_DIR`, kept beside `campaign.sh`,
which runs a build's `matisse_bench`, `vendor_bench` and `vendors.py` under `o200k,llama3`, the bpe crate and
tiktoken-rs under `o200k` alone, and `o200k_llama3_tables.py` writes its tables; the mixed file's ids beside it are

```
$ ./build/tools/probes/matisse_bench parallel VOCAB o200k,llama3 1 LARGE/mixed-2g.txt
```

The streaming section's session is `streaming.sh MATISSE OUTPUT_DIR`, kept beside `campaign.sh`, which runs a build's
`matisse_bench stream` under each tokenizer on each corpus, and `streaming_tables.py` writes its tables, and the
mutants' script and results are archived with the session; one corpus is

```
$ taskset -c 2 ./build/tools/probes/matisse_bench stream VOCAB cl100k 15 CORPORA/twitter.json 64 4096
```


The profile is

```
$ taskset -c 2 perf record -e cpu-clock -F 4999 --call-graph fp -o run.data \
      ./build/tools/probes/matisse_bench parallel VOCAB cl100k 4 CORPORA/wiki-zh.txt 1
$ perf script -i run.data -F ip,dso,dsoff | python3 -I profile_share.py
```

on a build configured with `-DCMAKE_CXX_FLAGS="-g -fno-omit-frame-pointer"`, `profile_share.py` beside the harnesses.
